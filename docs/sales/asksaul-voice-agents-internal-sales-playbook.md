# AskSaul Voice Agents — Internal Sales Playbook

**Audience:** Gregory + humans selling Saul  
**Objective:** Get qualified operators onto a demo fast, show immediate ROI, and close annual recurring revenue without overexplaining AI.  
**Core rule:** Speed to close beats feature education.

---

## 1. The sales thesis

Saul is not “AI phone software.” Saul is a revenue-protection layer for businesses where one missed call can become a lost job, lost patient, lost client, lost tenant, or lost emergency.

Most owners do not wake up wanting a voice agent. They wake up tired of:

- missing calls after hours,
- paying humans to answer repetitive questions,
- losing leads to voicemail,
- forgetting follow-up,
- having call details scattered across phones, texts, sticky notes, and CRMs.

Our job is to make the pain visible, then invite them to hear Saul live.

**Positioning line:**

> Saul covers the phones, captures the opportunity, and hands your team the next move.

**Cheeky simplicity line:**

> Three packages. No robot circus. Saul keeps it simple because your phones already make enough noise.

---

## 2. What we are really selling

We are selling **speed, coverage, and clean handoff**.

- **Speed:** every caller gets answered now, not tomorrow.
- **Coverage:** after-hours, overflow, weekends, busy moments, staff gaps.
- **Clean handoff:** call summary, contact details, urgency, intent, CRM/logging, Slack/morning brief, escalation rules.
- **Improvement loop:** humans review what Saul captures so the system gets sharper over time.

Do not lead with model names, prompt engineering, or technical stack unless the buyer asks.

Lead with business outcomes:

- “You stop losing calls to voicemail.”
- “Your team wakes up to qualified opportunities, not mystery missed calls.”
- “Saul costs less than one fully loaded phone hire and answers when that hire cannot.”

## 5. ROI math for sales conversations

Use simple math. Do not overpromise exact revenue.

### Human cost comparison

A fully loaded phone employee often costs:

- $18/hr ≈ **$3,900/mo**
- $22/hr ≈ **$4,767/mo**
- $28/hr ≈ **$6,067/mo**
- $35/hr ≈ **$7,583/mo**

And that person still does not reliably cover nights, weekends, overflow, sick days, vacations, or simultaneous calls.

### Revenue leakage prompt

Ask:

> “If Saul saved only 5 qualified calls per month, what would that be worth to you?”

Then calculate:

- Number of missed qualified calls/month
- Average job/client value
- Close rate
- Lifetime value if repeat business matters

Formula:

> Missed qualified calls × close rate × average value = monthly leakage

Example:

- 20 missed qualified calls/month
- 25% close rate
- $1,500 average job
- = **$7,500/month in potential leakage**

Night Manager at $2,500/mo only needs to help recover a fraction of that to make sense.

## 6. Fast qualification framework

Use this on the first call or DM.

### The 7 questions

1. How many inbound calls do you get in a normal month?
2. What happens when a call comes in after hours?
3. How many calls go to voicemail or get missed each week?
4. What is a qualified caller worth if they become a customer?
5. Where should call notes go today — CRM, Slack, text, email, spreadsheet?
6. What calls should Saul escalate to a human immediately?
7. If we could launch a safe first version in 1–2 weeks, who needs to approve it?

### Package fit

- **Operator:** low complexity, 50–200 calls/mo, after-hours capture.
- **Night Manager:** most SMBs, up to ~400 calls/mo, qualification/routing needed.
- **CCO:** 500+ calls/mo, multi-location, emergency routing, database/webhooks, sales agent, BDR/CSR replacement or support.

## 7. Demo-close call structure

### Goal

Get them to experience Saul, then convert the conversation into a scoped implementation.

### 15-minute demo agenda

1. **Pain confirmation — 3 minutes**
   - “Where are calls leaking today?”
   - “What happens after hours?”
   - “What is one missed good call worth?”

2. **Live Saul moment — 5 minutes**
   - Let them hear Saul.
   - Show what gets captured.
   - Show the morning brief / CRM-style handoff concept.

3. **ROI mirror — 3 minutes**
   - “If we captured 5–10 of these calls a month, this pays for itself.”
   - Compare against human phone cost.

4. **Package recommendation — 2 minutes**
   - Recommend one package. Do not show all options unless needed.

5. **Close — 2 minutes**
   - “If we can have this live in about two weeks, is there any reason not to start with the Night Manager package?”

## 8. Objection handling

### “Is this going to sound robotic?”

> That is exactly why we want you on a demo. Do not judge voice agents by old phone trees. Hear Saul, then decide. The goal is not to trick people. The goal is to answer quickly, collect the right details, and get your team the next move.

### “We already have someone answering phones.”

> Perfect. Saul is not trying to replace your best human. Saul covers what humans miss: after-hours, overflow, repetitive questions, and clean call summaries. Your team handles the valuable human moments; Saul keeps the leak from becoming invisible.

### “We do not want AI talking to customers.”

> Then start with after-hours intake and callback capture. Saul can be transparent, limited, and safe. The first version does not need to dispatch, quote, or make promises. It just answers, captures, and routes.

### “That sounds expensive.”

> Compared to what? One fully loaded phone hire can run $4,000–$7,500/month before nights and weekends. One missed high-value customer can cost more than Saul. We only need to recover a handful of qualified calls for this to make sense.

### “Can we try it month-to-month?”

> Yes. Month-to-month includes onboarding because the build work is real. If you sign for 12 months, we waive onboarding and protect your rate.

### “We need to think about it.”

> Totally fair. Before you think about a generic AI project, let’s make it concrete: what happens to calls tonight after 6 PM? If the answer is voicemail, we should at least map the call flow and show you the first safe version.

## 9. Internal do/don’t

### Do

- Push for demo quickly.
- Recommend Night Manager as the flagship default.
- Use revenue leakage math.
- Compare to fully loaded human phone cost.
- Sell safe first deployment: answer, intake, summarize, route.
- Use onboarding waiver to secure annual agreements.
- Keep Enterprise custom and anchored high.

### Don’t

- Lead with technical jargon.
- Show too many prices at once.
- Promise Saul can fully replace every CSR immediately.
- Let custom discovery drag on unpaid.
- Overbuild before a signed agreement.
- Let onboarding fees block a good annual deal.
